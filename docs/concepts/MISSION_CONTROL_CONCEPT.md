# Mission Control Concept

## Route

`/concepts/mission-control`

## Purpose

Mission Control is the isolated, mock-only executive overview of the entire business. It answers: “How is my business doing today?”

Mission Control prioritizes and routes. It does not explain why an individual Offer is performing a certain way and never replaces an Offer, Customer, or Order Workspace.

## Simplified Executive Experience

The experience uses one aggregate business trend, one Business Context launch strip, one daily briefing, one Attention Required hierarchy, and one concise list of winners. Every visible element reassures, warns, recommends, or routes.

Repeated summaries, scattered Intelligence callouts, and Offer-level investigative detail are intentionally excluded.

## Overall Business Trend

One hero graph represents the entire business. It defaults to Overall Profit and supports mock 7-day, 14-day, 30-day, 90-day, and yearly ranges. Profit, Revenue, Spend, Orders, Customers, ROAS, and CPA are mutually exclusive measure toggles so only one graph is visible at a time.

## Business Context Launch Strip

Business Context is secondary and appears beneath the aggregate trend. Each launch point includes a mock logo mark, name, Today's Profit, explicit trend direction, and labeled Tracking Health. Selection opens the Offer Workspace; it does not filter Mission Control.

## Today's Briefing

One briefing summarizes two Opportunities, one Warning, and one Recommendation. Opening it reveals a lightweight Drawer containing the observation, reason, recommendation, Explain, Evidence, and destination Workspace. Contextual Intelligence remains inside investigation Workspaces rather than being scattered through Mission Control.

## Attention Required

Attention Required is the primary content section and executive launch point. Every item identifies the Business, observation, business impact, severity, destination Workspace, Explain, and Evidence. The mock priorities include Shipping Margin decline, CPA increase, degraded Tracking, highest weekly Profit, and improving Affiliate Customer quality.

## Today's Winners

One concise ranked list shows Highest Profit, Highest ROAS, Lowest CPA, Highest LTV, Best Traffic Source, and Most Improved Offer. It is deliberately presented as a compact list rather than a KPI card wall.

## Continue Where You Left Off

A realistic Bullseye Shipping Margin investigation resumes directly in the Offer Workspace.

## Drawers

Mission Control Drawers answer: “Why should I investigate this?” They contain enough context to decide whether to proceed, then route into the appropriate Workspace. They do not reproduce Workspace analysis.

## Universal Search and MCP Chat

Command+K or Control+K opens mock Universal Search and routes results into the appropriate concept Workspace. MCP Chat remains a secondary, reactive Core entry point and remains separate from the proactive daily briefing.

## Recent Activity

Recent Activity and Recent Searches resume mock Offer, Customer, Order, Connector, and Evidence paths through the existing concept routes.

## Accessibility

Status uses icons, text, labels, signs, shape, and contrast. Trend direction and Tracking Health remain understandable without color. Native links, buttons, keyboard search, visible focus behavior, and semantic headings support keyboard and assistive-technology use.

## Responsive Behavior

Desktop uses the shared TraceKit navigation–experience–Drawer shell. Navigation and the Drawer become full-width temporary surfaces on narrow screens. Trend controls and the Business Context strip remain usable, attention items stack, and the executive hierarchy is preserved without creating a mobile card wall.

## Mock-Only Assumptions

All trends, ranges, Business Context metrics, Business Health, priorities, winners, briefings, activity, searches, Tracking Health, Evidence, and routes are local concept data. No live APIs, authentication, Connectors, persistence, production navigation, or production behavior are used.
